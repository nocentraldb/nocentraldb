# What a relay keeps: a data sheet for relay operators

This page is for whoever runs a NoCentralDB relay: the project for its own relay, or an app that deploys the `Relay` construct in its own AWS account ([Deploying the relay to AWS](relay-deploy.md)). It lists what the relay stores or sees, why, how long it keeps it by default, and where an operator changes that. Use it as the starting point for your privacy notice and your record of processing activities.

**This page is not legal advice.** It states what the design does, taken from the RFCs, so that you can adapt it: replace the defaults with the settings you deploy, add what you put around the relay (an authorizer, a proxy, your own logs), and have someone qualified decide what your notices must say. Who is controller and who is processor, the legal basis and the rights you offer are yours to decide; no RFC decides them.

The page decides nothing. Each row cites the RFC text it comes from. Where no RFC text decides a question, [Where the RFCs say nothing yet](#where-the-rfcs-say-nothing-yet) says so. It covers the AWS backend, the only one whose retention an RFC states ([RFC 0007, "The AWS backend"](rfc/0007-relay.md#the-aws-backend)).

## What the operator never sees

The relay holds no keys. Neither the relay, nor its operator, nor AWS, nor any backup sees **content** or a **key** that decrypts it: no object, field name, value, rich text or personal field, no space key and no person key. Changes arrive encrypted and signed; key envelopes and person-key records arrive wrapped, and the relay has no key to unwrap them. Every check the relay runs works on public keys, signatures, hashes and the readable bodies of control changes ([RFC 0007, "What the relay checks"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-the-relay-checks); [RFC 0007, "What the relay sees"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-the-relay-sees)). It cannot see the order in which changes were written, which objects or fields a change touches, or the app's model ([RFC 0002, "What the relay can see, and what it cannot"](rfc/0002-change-and-log-format.md#what-the-relay-can-see-and-what-it-cannot)). This is the first principle of the design ([RFC 0001, "The design in one page"](rfc/0001-nocentraldb.md#the-design-in-one-page)).

What the relay does see is **metadata**, and some of it is personal data: a member id stands for a person's device or agent, the member certificates link one account's devices across spaces, and a network address is personal data under the GDPR ([RFC 0007, "What the relay sees"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-the-relay-sees)). The table below lists it.

| **Member keys and certificates**: each member's public signing and agreement keys and its member certificate, so which devices and agents belong to one account, across every space on the relay. | To authenticate each connection by a challenge the member signs, and to verify the signature of every change. | As long as the space's log: the `members` table is written only from the log, and a former member keeps its row. | No setting. | [RFC 0007, "Opening a connection"](rfc/0007-relay.md#opening-a-connection); [RFC 0007, "Tables and keys"](rfc/0007-relay.md#tables-and-keys) |
| **Memberships**: which member is in which space, its role and when it changes, the cutoff of every revocation, the key envelopes addressed to each member and their generations, and the person-key ids an erasure destroyed. | To apply the membership rules: who may read and write a space, and which shred is valid. | As long as the space's log. | No setting. | [RFC 0007, "What the relay sees"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-the-relay-sees); [RFC 0008, "How the relay applies the rules"](rfc/0008-membership.md#how-the-relay-applies-the-rules) |
| **Admissions and the allowlist**: per account identity (a recovery key), its admission's until and spaces, the admission key that signed it, the count of spaces it created, the highest account key statement number seen, and a guest account's one space. | To decide who may create a space, so the relay does not become free storage for strangers. | Until the operator deletes the record. | **Encrypted changes**, with the clear parts of each: the space, the author's member id and sequence number, the key generation, the size of the frame and the time the relay accepted it. | To hand a space's changes to its members from a cursor; a member that joins pulls the whole log to decrypt the history. | As long as the relay keeps the space. There is no relay-side compaction yet, so storage grows with every change. | No setting. | [RFC 0007, "What the relay sees"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-the-relay-sees); [RFC 0007, "What it does not do"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-it-does-not-do) |
| **Uploads of large changes**: a frame too large for one message, with its space, member and size. | To carry a change that does not fit in one message through S3. | An upload URL is valid 15 minutes, a download URL 5 minutes. An upload is deleted once a push takes it, and a lifecycle rule deletes uploads older than one day. The frame then stays in the log like any other. | No setting. | [RFC 0007, "How large changes go to S3"](rfc/0007-relay.md#how-large-changes-go-to-s3) |
| **Wrapped person-key records**, with the person-key id, the generation and the member id that wrote each. They show how many people have personal data in a space, never who. | So members find the key to each person's fields without reading the whole log (the person-key store). | Until the relay accepts the shred entry that erases the person; a provisional erasure deletes it no sooner than 7 days after the shred. | No setting: only a shred entry deletes a record. | [RFC 0007, "Keys and the person-key store"](rfc/0007-relay.md#keys-and-the-person-key-store) |
| **Connection records**: which member id was connected when, and to which spaces it subscribed. | To deliver new changes and `added` to the open connections of a space's members. | Removed when the connection closes. A TTL removes what a missed close leaves, and API Gateway closes every connection after 2 hours. An operator may keep records after the connection ends, for abuse investigation, for a period it chooses: RFC 0007 suggests 30 days. Kept records are never read to deliver changes, and the TTL deletes them when the period ends. | `settings.keepConnectionRecordsDays` of the `Relay` construct: the days records are kept after the connection ends. Left out, none are kept. | [RFC 0007, "What the relay sees"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-the-relay-sees); [RFC 0007, "Tables and keys"](rfc/0007-relay.md#tables-and-keys) |
| **Network addresses**, and the client string and protocol versions a member sends, which together can fingerprint a device. | API Gateway holds each connection from its address; the client string helps debug version problems. | Seen while the connection lasts. An address is written down only if API Gateway access logging is on and its format names the source address; it is off in the `Relay` construct. | API Gateway access logging on the stage, and its log format and retention. | [RFC 0007, "What the relay sees"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-the-relay-sees); [RFC 0007, "Opening a connection"](rfc/0007-relay.md#opening-a-connection); the construct's default: [What the relay logs](relay-deploy.md#what-the-relay-logs-and-what-to-leave-off-around-it) |
| **The relay's logs**: member ids, space ids, change ids, sizes, error codes and times. Never a frame, a payload, a key envelope, a person-key record or other key material. | Not stated (see below). | 14 days, or any longer period the operator sets. | `settings.logRetentionDays` of the `Relay` construct: 14 when left out, or a longer period CloudWatch Logs offers. | [RFC 0007, "What the relay sees"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-the-relay-sees); [RFC 0007, "Services"](rfc/0007-relay.md#services) |
| **Backups**: earlier states of every table through DynamoDB point-in-time recovery, including person-key records a shred deleted, still wrapped. S3 versioning is off. | To bring back the membership table and the person-key store after a loss, before any member reconnects. | 35 days. A restore ends with a new relay epoch and re-applies every shred in the restored log. | No setting in the construct for point-in-time recovery. S3 versioning is a setting of the bucket; turned on, it keeps deleted frames until their versions expire. | [RFC 0007, "Restores and the relay epoch"](rfc/0007-relay.md#restores-and-the-relay-epoch); [RFC 0003, "Shredding a person's key"](rfc/0003-keys-and-cryptography.md#shredding-a-persons-key) |
| **AWS API calls** in CloudTrail: which role or function called which AWS API. They name the relay's functions, not members. | Recorded by AWS for the account. | Management events as the account's CloudTrail keeps them. Data events only if the operator turns them on. | The account's CloudTrail settings. | [RFC 0007, "What the relay sees"](rfc/0007-relay.md#what-the-relay-sees) |

Erasure reaches personal fields, not this metadata. Erasing a person destroys their person key, so their fields become unreadable everywhere the key is gone; the member ids, sizes and times above stay ([RFC 0001, "The team lead who has to pass the data-protection review"](rfc/0001-nocentraldb.md#the-team-lead-who-has-to-pass-the-data-protection-review)). A member of the space who obtains a backup taken before the erasure can still open that person's record ([RFC 0003, "Shredding a person's key"](rfc/0003-keys-and-cryptography.md#shredding-a-persons-key)).

## Where the RFCs say nothing yet

These questions have no RFC text yet, or RFC text the relay does not build yet. The page leaves each of them open, and a notice built on it should too:

- **How long a space's log, its memberships and its member keys are kept.** No RFC sets an end, no request deletes a space, and the compaction RFC is not written. An operator cannot yet say when this metadata goes (NOC-533).
- **Metadata of a person who asks for erasure.** Erasure covers personal fields. No RFC says whether or how an operator removes a former member's member id, certificate, and the sizes and times of its changes from a space's log (NOC-526).
- **When an admission record goes.** An admission lapses, but no RFC says when its record, the count of spaces, the statement number or a guest's one-space record are deleted, nor how an operator deletes one when RFC 0015 asks it to (NOC-526).
- **What the relay's logs are for.** RFC 0007 states what they hold and how long, not their purpose (NOC-533).
- **Backends other than AWS.** RFC 0007 states retention only for the AWS backend; the SQLite relay server and the Postgres backend have none (NOC-533).
