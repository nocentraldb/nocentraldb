# 2026-11-23 · NOC-421 · The parts of a split write depend each on the one before and fold on the writer in one SQLite transaction

- Decided by: coding agent (NOC-421)
- Why: the owner's decision keeps a split write atomic on the writing device. The core seals every part first, each part's dependencies being the part before it (the first part's being the heads), with consecutive author sequences, and `View::receive_all_own_changes` appends and folds all of them in one SQLite transaction, without decoding their frames again (NOC-422), so a crash or a failure leaves none of them, and a subscriber hears of the write once. The chain makes every other member fold the parts in the order they were written. A part's budget is the 16 MiB limit less a room of 1.5 KiB for the header, signature, AEAD nonce and tag and padding, and less the payload's other keys counted for the heads the write may have; a test seals a part filled to its budget and checks its frame. A write that fits into one change keeps its operations in the order the transaction made them; a larger one groups them by object, in the order each object first appears.
- Instead of: folding each part in its own SQLite transaction through the single-change path, which a crash between two parts would leave half written on the writer; sealing each part after folding the one before, which needs the same transaction to stay open across sealing.
- Topics: core, sdk-rust, limits
