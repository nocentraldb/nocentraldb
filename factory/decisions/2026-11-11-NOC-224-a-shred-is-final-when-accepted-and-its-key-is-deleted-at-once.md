# 2026-11-11 · NOC-224 · A shred is final when accepted, and members and the relay delete its key at once

- Decided by: Carsten, 2026-11-11, on NOC-224 (option 6)
- Why: an admin device that is stolen and not yet revoked could reject a shred that was already stable, by a fork below a counted sequence or by a fresh change that claims not to have seen it, after its key was destroyed; erasure cannot be undone. A shred's verdict now reads only its causal past: its author's right to perform an erasure and the sign-off account's admin member (RFC 0008, step 1). Revocations do not exclude a shred, and no later change flips it, so every copy reaches the same verdict as soon as it holds the shred and the key is deleted at once. The sign-off by a second account with an admin member is the whole defence against a malicious erasure; a thief who also controls such an account can erase one person's data, a loss of data, not a leak. NOC-229 writes it into RFC 0008 and the code; the owner's addition for spaces with one admin account is NOC-228.
- Instead of: refusing a second change at a counted sequence (1), which breaks convergence; treating an admin's equivocation as its revocation (2) or a per-author chain rule (3), which a thief turns against honest changes; bounding a revocation's cutoff by its causal past (4), which leaves admins that are not revoked open; or holding every deletion until something stronger (5), which never completes the erasure.
