# 2026-11-11 · NOC-196 · The erasure sign-off reads every certificate in the causal past for recovery keys, so a statement can only block it

- Decided by: the owner on NOC-201 (A, 2026-11-11); pm, within A, that every certificate counts (NOC-196)
- Why: the owner chose the safe subset: a sign-off counts only if its account differs from the performer's by account key and by every recovery key that certificates in the causal past attach to either account key; a shared recovery key means the same account. The owner's text says "any admin certificate". pm read it as every certificate of the shred's causal past, current or former member, the performer's included, since A's intent is fail-closed and each extra certificate can only block a sign-off. The fold reads the certificates through `Resolved::certificates`, and `same_account` in `membership/rights.rs` compares the two account keys' recovery-key sets. A certificate in the state carries only a statement that names its own key 1 and verifies (`Certificate::verify`), so a recovery key attaches to the certificate's own account key.
- Instead of: only certificates of members who are admins in the causal past, the owner's words read narrowly, which would miss the performer's own statement or a former admin's; comparing by recovery key alone (NOC-201 C), fail-open on an irreversible action; waiting for NOC-187 (B).
- What this does not do: until NOC-187 carries newer statements into spaces, a person's account keys before and after a recovery count as two accounts unless a certificate in the space attaches the same recovery key to both. RFC 0008 says so.
