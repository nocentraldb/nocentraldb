# 2026-11-12 · NOC-273 · The pairing answer is `ncdb-pair-1:` and a fixed binary layout, the account space has a model the SDK ships, and a link statement is CBOR signed by both keys

- Decided by: Carsten (2026-11-12, on NOC-273, questions 5 to 7, A each)
- Why: the pairing answer uses the invitation's scheme: `ncdb-pair-1:` followed by a fixed layout in base64url (about 390 characters for a device, 285 for an agent, fits a QR code) holding the certificate, the account key statement, for a device the account key sealed to it, and when a database with its own account is linked, the account's half of the link statement. The account space's model is shipped by the SDK with its own `model.id`, so every app of one account shares one account space; its collections are `members`, `memberships`, `links`, `statements` and `wrappedKeys`, ids and sealed bytes stored as text, its tables under reserved names starting with `$`, reached only through `db.account()`. The link statement is a small signed CBOR record under `"nocentraldb/account-link"` naming the old account key and the new account's identity; the new account's half travels in the pairing answer, the joining database signs the old half just before deleting its old key, a device writes it into the account space, and an agent keeps it.
- Instead of: a CBOR map for the pairing answer (5B), longer and with more than one way to write it, as for the invitation (NOC-141); merging the account collections into the app's model (6B), so two apps of one person could not share an account space, or keeping them outside the view (6C), a second storage path; a link signed by the old key only (7B), so anybody could claim another account, or links for devices only (7C), so an agent's old memberships show as an unknown account.
- Topics: accounts, pairing, linking, formats, rfc-0010
