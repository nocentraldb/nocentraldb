# 2026-11-10 · NOC-86 · Who may hold a generation, for the exposure rule

- Decided by: coding agent (NOC-86)
- Why: RFC 0008 (step 4 of "Two removals that rotate the key at once") names who may hold a generation: the author of its key chain entry, every recipient of an envelope for it, accepted or rejected, and whoever may hold a generation that descends from it; a generation is exposed when one of them is not a member. Where it leaves a detail open: (1) the creator holds the first generation, which has no key chain entry; (2) the authors counted are those of every key chain entry the graph holds, valid when written whatever its final verdict (NOC-71); an entry that was never valid when written adds no generation, and its author made up its own key, so it is not counted; (3) an envelope's author is not counted on its own, as RFC 0008 lists only recipients; an admin that wraps a key holds it through its own entry or envelope; (4) holders pass from a generation to each of its previous generations, since the key chain opens them. They are recorded per generation in `Generation::holders`, which the state's canonical encoding carries, as RFC 0008's "The state" asks.
- Instead of: counting only accepted envelopes, the broken fixture M-C12 names; or counting authors of entries that were never valid, which would let a removed member force rotations by naming the current generation.
- Topics: rfc-0008, keys, membership, convergence
