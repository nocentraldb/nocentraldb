# 2026-11-10 · NOC-191 · A certificate's key 6 is checked with the certificate, and a statement reads strictly

- Decided by: coding agent (NOC-191), inside the core, where RFC 0003 leaves the check's place open
- Why: RFC 0003 ("Member certificate") says a member checks a chain of two, the statement against its recovery key and the certificate against the account key the statement names. `Certificate::verify` therefore also requires, when key 6 is present, that the statement's signature verifies strictly with its key 1 and that its key 2 equals the certificate's key 1; the genesis and add checks of the fold call `verify`, so every copy and the relay refuse such a certificate alike. A certificate without key 6 verifies as before. A statement decodes only with keys 1 to 4, fixed lengths and a number of at least 1 ("1 for the first account key"), and `verify` refuses number 0 too, since the fields are public; a malformed key 6 refuses the certificate as `InvalidValue(6)`. Whether the recovery key is the account the member expects, and whether a newer statement replaced this one, stay the SDK's checks before it writes, as RFC 0010 ("Losing a device") says; comparing accounts by recovery key for the erasure sign-off (RFC 0010's change to RFC 0008, decision M9) is not built here.
- Instead of: decoding key 6 without checking it, which would let a certificate carry a statement for another account; checking the statement only in the SDK, which would make the fold's verdict differ from what RFC 0003 says a member checks.
- Topics: crypto, membership, rfc-0003, rfc-0010
