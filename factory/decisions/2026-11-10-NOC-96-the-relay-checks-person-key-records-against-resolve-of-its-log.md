# 2026-11-10 · NOC-96 · The relay checks person-key records against `resolve` of its log, one request at a time

- Decided by: coding agent (NOC-96)
- Why: RFC 0007 accepts a record from a current member when its writer's signature verifies, an entry in the log announces its generation and record hash, no shred destroyed its id, and its generation is the stored record's or descends from it. The relay core reads these from `resolve` over the space's control changes, as it does for a control change (NOC-87): the writer's key from its member state, current or former; the announcing entry and the shred from the person keys of accepted changes only; descent from the generation graph. `put-person-keys` is all or nothing: one record that fails answers `bad_record` and stores none, and the records of one request are checked in order, each against the one before it of the same id. A record that does not read as `{1: person-key id (16 bytes), 2: generation, 3: writer (32 bytes), 4: wrapped, 5: signature}` with no other key makes the request `malformed`. `keys` serves every key envelope addressed to the member and every key chain entry the log holds, whatever their verdict, since the member folds them itself. RFC 0007 pages neither `keys` nor `person-keys`; an answer above one 128 KiB message answers `too_large`.
- Instead of: a person-key table the backends keep beside the membership table, which every backend would have to keep in step with `resolve`; storing the good records of a request with a bad one, which leaves the member unsure what was stored; or serving only accepted envelopes, which a later control change can flip.
- What this does not do: a put costs a read of the space's control changes; two puts of one id at the same time are not ordered against each other, so the later write wins even when its generation does not descend from the earlier's (NOC-118); a space with more records or envelopes than fit into one message cannot be served whole.
