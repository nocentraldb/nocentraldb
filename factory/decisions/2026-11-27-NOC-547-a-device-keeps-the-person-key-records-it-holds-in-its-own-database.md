# 2026-11-27 · NOC-547 · A device keeps the person-key records it holds in its own database, and a reopen holds their keys again

- Decided by: coding agent (NOC-547), pending the owner's review of the on-disk addition
- Why: person keys were held in memory only (NOC-206), so a database reopened without a relay held none, and every personal field read as unset; a key drawn without a relay was never stored anywhere and was lost at the first close. The device now keeps each record it accepts, as the relay stores it (person-key id, generation, writer, the key wrapped under that generation's space key, the writer's signature), in the table `person_key_records` beside the view, and on open accepts them again with the same checks as a fetched record. A record this member wrote waits in the table, also across a reopen, until a relay took it or refused it with `bad_record`; syncing starts with the waiting records, so a record written without a relay, or while it was out of reach, reaches the relay once the device connects. The space keys come back from the envelopes in the log, so nothing new is in the clear on the disk. A shred that destroys a key removes its records (`secure_delete` overwrites them). `SpaceStatus::missing_person_keys` (`missingPersonKeys`) counts the keys the log announces, not destroyed, that the device does not hold.
- Instead of: keeping the person keys themselves in the platform keystore or the key file, a second key store to keep in step with shreds and rotations; or keeping keys in memory and only reporting them as missing, which loses every key drawn without a relay at the next close.
- What this does not do: a key drawn without a relay before this change and lost at a close does not come back; its values read as unset, and the status counts the key.
