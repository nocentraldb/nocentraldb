# 2026-11-27 · NOC-547 · A device keeps the person-key records it holds in its own database, and a reopen holds their keys again

- Decided by: coding agent (NOC-547), pending the owner's review of the on-disk addition
- Why: person keys were held in memory only (NOC-206), so a database reopened without a relay held none, and every personal field read as unset; a key drawn without a relay was never stored anywhere and was lost at the first close. The device now keeps each record it accepts, as the relay stores it (person-key id, generation, writer, the key wrapped under that generation's space key, the writer's signature), in the table `person_key_records` beside the view, and on open accepts them again with the same checks as a fetched record. The space keys come back from the envelopes in the log, so nothing new is in the clear on the disk. A shred that destroys a key removes its records (`secure_delete` overwrites them). `SpaceStatus::missing_person_keys` (`missingPersonKeys`) counts the keys the log announces, not destroyed, that the device does not hold.
- Instead of: keeping the person keys themselves in the platform keystore or the key file, a second key store to keep in step with shreds and rotations; or keeping keys in memory and only reporting them as missing, which loses every key drawn without a relay at the next close.
- What this does not do: a key drawn without a relay before this change and lost at a close does not come back; its values read as unset, and the status counts the key. Records waiting to be put to a relay when the app closes are kept on the device, but are not put again after the reopen; another member gets such a key only once this one re-wraps it or puts it again.
