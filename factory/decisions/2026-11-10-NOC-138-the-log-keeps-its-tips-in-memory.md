# 2026-11-10 · NOC-138 · The log keeps its tips in memory, and rebuilds them after a reopen by opening each folded change once

- Decided by: coding agent (NOC-138)
- Why: a write took its heads, sequence and clock by reading and opening the whole log of its space (NOC-119). The core's `Log` is the one place every state change passes through, for the SDK's own writes, pulled changes, re-checked rejected changes and un-folds alike, so it keeps them as `Tips`: each author's highest sequence on append, and on each change of state the folded changes with their dependencies (a control change's from its body, a sealed change's from the payload the checks opened to fold it) and each folded sealed change's clock. A state the view reads back from its `log` table, after a reopen or a refold for a changed lock (NOC-148), comes without a payload, so `Log::tips` opens such a change once, the first time the tips are read. The tips stay in memory: the view's tables and the format version do not change. A test compares them with a full recount after every change in any order, through un-folds and folds again, and the SDK's next change with one over writes, pulls, an un-fold, a reopen and a refold.
- Instead of: keeping them in the SDK, which sees neither the changes sync pulls nor the un-folds a control change causes inside the core; or a table of dependencies in the view, a new on-disk format that would have to be proven equal to the log.
- What this does not do: the first write after the database opens still opens each folded sealed change of its space once. The log's frames stay in memory as before (NOC-69).
