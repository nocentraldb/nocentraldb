# 2026-11-13 · NOC-268 · The account space's statements and sealed keys have ids derived from what they record, and a sealed key is its encapsulated key then its ciphertext

- Decided by: Carsten, on the NOC-268 PR (pending tick)
- Why: RFC 0010's account space holds a `statements` object per account key statement and a `wrappedKeys` object per new account key and remaining device, but names neither their ids nor the bytes of `sealed`. Their ids derive from what they record, as the member and membership records' do (NOC-266): the first 16 bytes of SHA-256 over `"nocentraldb/account-statement"` and the number as 8 big-endian bytes, and over `"nocentraldb/account-wrapped-key"`, the device's 32-byte member id and the number. So every device derives the same id for the same record, and a second renewal with a number the account space already records is refused rather than written twice. `sealed` is unpadded base64url of HPKE's 32-byte encapsulated key followed by the 48-byte ciphertext of the account key's seed and its tag, sealed under RFC 0003's pairing-answer `info` `"nocentraldb/account-key"`: the two fields of the pairing answer at offsets 203 and 235, in the same order. A device that removes a lost member numbers the new account key one more than the statement it holds, which is the account's current one; recovery numbers it max(learned + 1, Unix seconds) because a recovering device holds none (NOC-285).
- Instead of: random object ids, which let two devices that renew at once record the same number twice; and a CBOR map for `sealed`, which RFC 0010 rules out by giving the field as base64url of the two values.
