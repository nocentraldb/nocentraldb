# Measurement and Brand Valuation

## Source abbreviations
- **KOB** = *Kellogg on Branding*, Tybout/Calkins (eds.), Kellogg School of Management, 2005.
- **TIM** = *This is Marketing*, Seth Godin, 2018.

## Character of the sources for this subject area
**THIS IS WHERE THE MAIN CONTRADICTION OF THE SYSTEM SITS.** Both books measure direct response and do not refuse to do so there; **the dispute concerns brand effect exclusively.**
**KOB is the denser source for methods**: the three-pathway model of brand measurement, four families of measurement for advertising effect, a perception-test procedure, seven internal brand metrics, and a worked-through brand valuation. **TIM is the denser source for funnel and lifetime value mathematics** — and supplies surprisingly robust, fully worked-through cascades. **The widespread assumption that TIM is unquantitative is wrong**; it holds only for the brand-effect side, where TIM explicitly refuses to measure.
**KOB is from 2005** and does not know today's attribution landscape. **TIM carries a conflict of interest** (seminar sales).

## Core concepts and definitions

### The dividing line that orders the whole dispute
- **Direct marketing is action-oriented. AND IT IS MEASURED.**
- **Brand marketing is culturally oriented. AND IT CANNOT BE MEASURED.** [Source: TIM, ch. 15; origin of the distinction: Lester Wunderman]
**The double rule that follows:** For direct marketing, "**measure everything** ... if you're not able to measure it, it doesn't count." For brand marketing, "**be patient. REFUSE TO MEASURE.** ... If you can't afford to be consistent and patient, **don't pay for brand marketing ads.**"
**The reality finding on the measurement rate:** When Godin started in marketing, an estimated **under 5 percent** of all advertisers measured their results (too difficult with TV, radio and print). **"Today, I'm guessing the number is closer to 60 PERCENT, because the numbers are so clearly marked. WHAT'S MISSING IS A THOUGHTFUL ANALYSIS OF WHAT THOSE NUMBERS MEAN."** [Source: TIM, ch. 19]
**KOB's counter-finding on the measurement rate at brand level:** A Prophet survey of **over 100 senior marketing and C-level decision-makers** in the late 1990s found that **only a third** of the participating companies measured their brand's performance at all. "**Those who don't measure their performance are missing out on reaping the most benefits from their brand strategies.**" [Source: KOB, ch. 12]

## Frameworks, models and methods

### The three-pathway model of brand measurement
| Pathway | What is measured | Data type | Methods |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Customer-Based Brand Metrics** | customers' knowledge and attitude | Attitudinal Data | Hierarchy of Effects, Tracking Studies |
| **2 Incremental Brand Sales** | short-term incremental sales or cash flows | Marketplace Performance Data | Marketing Mix Modeling (ROI), Predictive Modeling (ROCI) |
| **3 Branded Business Value** | financial value of the brand to the firm | Brand Valuation | Discounted Cash Flows, Brand Scorecards |
**The demarcation: "Pathways 1 and 2 are essentially tools for judging the EFFECTIVENESS of various [programmes]"** — pathway 3 values the brand itself. And the closing finding: **three separate, distinct pathways that answer different questions.** [Source: KOB, ch. 13]

**PATHWAY 1 — Customer-Based Brand Metrics.** "**Customer-based brand equity is the differential effect brand knowledge [has].**" From the mid-1980s onwards, finance and marketing managers began to pay attention to it.
Tool example **BrandDynamics** (Millward Brown) with the ascending pyramid **Presence -> Relevance -> Performance -> Advantage -> Bonding** and the associated customer questions: "**Do I know it?**", "**Does it offer me something?**", "**Does it offer me something better?**", "**Nothing else beats it.**" The printed diagnostic example shows a brand that **wins on Presence (+35) but loses on Advantage (−12) and Bonding (−14)** — that is, awareness without attachment.
**The three explicitly named assumptions, i.e. the built-in weak points of this pathway:** the basic assumption of the approach itself; the precondition that **strong feelings** lead to behaviour; and the precondition that marketers can determine the value drivers at all. [Source: KOB, ch. 13]

**PATHWAY 2 — Incremental Brand Sales.** Two methods:
- **Marketing Mix Modeling** determines the **historical** brand return. Procedure: "baselining" — the separation of **base dollars** and **incremental dollars**. A printed example measures media and promotion effect and explicitly notes for one channel **"Radio | Impact too small"**.
- **Predictive Modeling** forecasts future returns via **ROCI (Return-on-Customer-Investment).**
**The core formula: CBV = P × BR × SOP × M**, where CBV = Customer-Brand Value and **M = Margin** (the brand's gross contribution margin).
**Four steps:** (1) determine the value of the customers to the brand. (2) aggregate customers by value or target. (3) use a **closed-loop brand investment process**: *Present Customer Value in Dollars -> Marketer-Controlled Message/Incentive AND Uncontrollable Messages from Environment -> Measurable Customer Behavior in Dollars -> New Return Measures.* "**The importance of the closed-loop process is that IT USES A FINANCIAL YARDSTICK.**" (4) develop a probability model.
**The 14-row ROCI table**, separated into **Present Users / Competitive Users / New and Emerging Users**:
1 Present Income Flow in Category · 2 Share of Requirements · 3 Customer Income Flow to Brand · 4 Contribution Margin (%) · 5 Contribution Margin ($) · **6 Income Flow WITHOUT brand communication programme · 7 Contribution Margin WITHOUT programme · 8 Income Flow WITH programme · 9 Gross Contribution Margin WITH programme** · 10 Brand Communication Investment · 11 Net Contribution Margin · 12 Difference in contribution margins · **13 Incremental Gain or Loss · 14 Return on Investment (%)**.
**The methodological core of this table: it sets the state WITH and WITHOUT brand communication side by side and makes the difference the measured quantity** — that is, control-group logic at portfolio level. [Source: KOB, ch. 13]

**PATHWAY 3 — Branded Business Value.** Three objects of valuation: **trademark valuation** (the value of a mark), brand valuation, and **branded business valuation** (corporate).
**The four-step methodology (Brand Finance):**
1. **Financial forecasts** from financial and market data.
2. **Brand Value Added (BVA) — the brand's contribution to demand**, determined via demand drivers. **The decisive range: "value can be as low as 1 PERCENT (or less) of revenue for a commoditized, industrialized product, or 50 TO 70 PERCENT (or more) for a well-known luxury brand."**
3. **Brand beta analysis** — the risk factors, i.e. the probability that the forecast will come true.
4. **Applying the discount rate and estimating the brand value**, plus sensitivity analysis.
**The worked-through example (Discounted Future Earnings Method, durable goods, simplified):**
Net sales **500** rising to **650** over five years; operating profit **75.0** in the base year rising to **97.5**; tangible capital employed **250 to 325**, charged at **5 percent per year**; deducting the charge gives intangible earnings of **62.5** to **77.5**; of these, **25 percent is attributed to the brand**, i.e. **15.6** in the base year rising to **20.3**; a **tax rate of 33 percent** gives post-tax earnings of **10.5** rising to **13.6**; a **discount rate of 15 percent** with factors from 1.15 in year 1 to 2.01 in year 5 gives discounted cash flows of **9.5 falling to 6.8**; sum of the five years **50.8**, plus annuity **45.1** at 0 percent growth — **estimated brand value 95.9.**
**The reading note on it:** The two levers that dominate the result are **the brand's share of intangible earnings (here 25 percent)** and **the discount rate (here 15 percent)**. Both are judgements, not measurements. [Source: KOB, ch. 13]

### Four families of measurement for advertising effect — with their limits
**Common practice (copy testing):** Consumers are recruited to watch TV programming into which test ads are inserted **alongside control ads**; brand preferences in several categories are collected beforehand. Sometimes **respondents turn a dial** to indicate their moment-to-moment interest. **The criticism of this: "recent research suggests that measuring whether interest INCREASES OR DECREASES over the full length of the ad, as well as measuring the LEVEL OF PEAK interest, may be more indicative of ad effectiveness than the specific response to particular portions of the copy."**
**(1) LEARNING / RECALL.** Advantage: "the intended outcome can be **unambiguously attributed** to the specific campaign." Two problems:
- **Underestimation: "People often have difficulty TRACING THE ORIGIN of their knowledge. People may have acquired information about a brand from being exposed to advertising, but they may not remember that the information came from a specific ad. As a result they do not report this information when asked to recall a message, WHICH LEADS TO AN UNDERESTIMATION OF LEARNING."** Solution: **brand recall** instead of **ad recall** — "people might be asked WHAT THEY KNOW ABOUT A BRAND", compared with a **control group** without exposure. "Such brand recall reflects what people know about the brand, **which is more likely to be predictive of their purchasing behavior** than a measure of what they can remember about an ad."
- **Wrong direction: "Consumers may exhibit GOOD RECALL of an automobile manufacturer's claim that a car comes with a complete tool kit BECAUSE THEY ASSOCIATE THIS CLAIM WITH THE THOUGHT THAT THE TOOL KIT WOULD BE NEEDED TO FIX THE CAR'S FREQUENT BREAKDOWNS. In this case, good advertising recall is associated with a DISINCLINATION TO PURCHASE."**
**(2) ATTITUDES.** Rating on affective items (like-dislike, superior-inferior) and brand-specific ones (value for money, effectiveness), collected **without reference to the advertising**, compared with a control group.
**The limit of use that is decisive for the system's contradiction: "Attitude measures are most useful when the brand is NEW or when the advertising presents significant NEWS. WHEN BRANDS ARE WELL-ESTABLISHED, IT IS UNLIKELY THAT ONE OR EVEN SEVERAL EXPOSURES TO ADVERTISING, WHICH ARE TYPICALLY USED IN TESTS OF EFFECTIVENESS, WILL AFFECT PEOPLE'S JUDGMENTS."**
**(3) IMPLICIT MEASURES (reaction time).** Procedure: the respondent has to recognise whether a string of letters is a word; **a logo or brand name (e.g. Pampers) is flashed subliminally beforehand.** "**The STRONGER the association between 'soft' and Pampers in the respondent's mind, THE FASTER the word 'soft' will be identified.**" Purpose: "**assess automatic, nonconscious attitudes THAT EVADE more traditional direct measures**" — needed when respondents do not disclose their true attitude because of social desirability or have no distinguishable preference.
**(4) BRAND-CHOICE INDICATORS.** Purchase probability on a scale; brand-switching potential as a change in purchase intention or preference.
**The decisive restriction: "For many ESTABLISHED brands, advertising is NOT likely to alter consumers' beliefs about the brand or their preferences. Nonetheless, advertising may serve as a REMINDER by enhancing the brand's TOP-OF-MIND awareness, and in turn increase brand choice probabilities."** Measurement: via a category cue ("which beer brands would you consider"), compared with a non-exposure group — "an increase is indicated by the observation that people who have seen an ad include this brand in their list **to a greater extent (or EARLIER in the list)**."
**And the assignment to the choice situation:** Top-of-mind recall suits **memory-based choice**; for **stimulus-based choice** you need perceptual measures — "**brand name identification** (respondents are asked to identify Bullseye, being presented very briefly, **50 msec**, on a computer screen), and **product recognition** (the time respondents take to pick out the brand from a **collage** of competing brands)."
**The chapter's closing recommendation: "In assessing the effectiveness of advertising, focus should center on WHAT PEOPLE KNOW ABOUT THE BRAND AND HOW THEY FEEL ABOUT THIS INFORMATION, RATHER THAN ON WHAT THEY CAN RECALL ABOUT THE ADVERTISING."** With an explicit plea for **greater reliance on implicit measures**, because they capture "what people have learned from the advertising **even though they may have forgotten where they got the information.**" [Source: KOB, ch. 7]

### The perception test for brand design — the most concrete test prescription in the system
**Basic rule: evaluate brand design and product design separately.** "Ultimately the two will be intertwined in the marketplace, **but they must be evaluated separately.**"
- **Product design:** trained panels, in environments that **eliminate** perceptual cues. "**The last thing we would want to do is to provide perceptual cues such as names if we wanted to focus on product design.**"
- **Brand design:** real consumers, minimise information about the physical product, **expose very briefly (a few seconds)** — "it is important that they react **in a perceptual mode — fast and automatically. MORE CONSIDERED RESPONSES TEND TO OBSCURE ACTUAL PERCEPTUAL RESPONSES.**"
**THE EXPLICIT CONDEMNATION OF A METHOD: "One of the WORST methods of brand design evaluation is to give consumers in a FOCUS GROUP (or in other types of open-ended interviews) a design and let them comment on it. Such settings are very likely to stimulate people to think about the design in all kinds of ways (INCLUDING PLAYING DESIGN EXPERT) that OBSCURE the design's real perceptual impact."**
**The recommended technique, in order of rank:** Show the design briefly, then measure attention to the cues by asking what consumers remember (names, colours). Alternatively, track **eye movements and fixations** (needs special equipment). **"Even more important than measuring what consumers LOOK AT, however, is to ask consumers to RATE the design, or specific cues, ON SEVEN-POINT SCALES THAT REFLECT THE DESIRED PERCEPTUAL CATEGORIES."** For Philadelphia, then: how rich, creamy and authentic do they think the product is? **Result: a perception profile across all target categories, "both useful EVALUATIVE and DIAGNOSTIC information."** [Source: KOB, ch. 2]

### Funnel and lifetime value mathematics
**The funnel cascade in a real case (Casey Neistat):** A video with about **1,000,000** views led to a Twitch livestream that was watched **18,000** times (**about one in fifty**); there were about **1,000** comments (**one in eighteen**); of the commenters, **perhaps five** will go further. **The chain: 1,000,000 → 18,000 → 1,000 → 5.** "That's what a funnel looks like. **Your mileage may vary.**"
**The interpretation that turns the optimisation question around: "The reason that he's Casey and we're not ISN'T BECAUSE HE HAS OPTIMIZED HIS FUNNEL. It's because the TOP of the funnel is regularly and effortlessly filled with people who are ENROLLED in his journey."**
**The direct-response cascade, worked through:** 1,000 USD for online ads to a million people → **twenty clicks** → **50 USD per click** → one in ten becomes an order → **500 USD per order.** If the lifetime value is above 500 USD, you can keep buying — "all the ads paying for themselves. **Magic!**"
**The critical addition on the distribution of value: "The vast majority of your profit is going straight from you to the place you're buying the ads ... They're SKIMMING THE PROFIT OFF THE TOP. GOOGLE MIGHT MAKE A HUNDRED DOLLARS PER SALE IN PROFIT, WHERE YOU, THE ADVERTISER DOING ALL THE WORK, ARE MAKING JUST TEN DOLLARS."** And the reality check: "**Very few organizations do this math carefully. They're SPENDING AND PRAYING.**"
**The lifetime value calculation (supermarket):** Profit on a single purchase only "a dollar or two", because supermarkets have very low margins. As a regular customer — **twice a week, 100 USD each time, over five years in the neighbourhood** — "that's **50,000 USD or more in revenue. Even at a 2 percent profit margin, it means A THOUSAND DOLLARS IN PROFIT from each new customer over time.**" And the amplification: "**What if your supermarket is special, and once someone becomes a shopper, there's a chance that they'll tell friends and neighbors and one of them will also become a regular? That makes each new customer even more valuable, because they become YOUR ENGINE OF GROWTH.**"
**The postage-stamp calculation as the simplest entry point:** 50 cents per letter, a thousand letters for one order = **500 USD per order.** At a lifetime value of **700 USD**: "buy as many stamps as you can possibly afford!" At **400 USD**: "you have no business buying stamps. **You need a better letter or a better business.**"
**The online transfer with an important tension: "Every click between the first and the last makes your funnel MORE EXPENSIVE, but IF YOU GET RID OF TOO MANY CLICKS THEN NO ONE WILL TRUST YOU ENOUGH TO BUY FROM YOU."**
**The two stop rules: "If you can't SEE the funnel, don't buy the ads. If you can MEASURE the funnel and it costs too much for you to afford ads, don't buy the ads. FIX THE FUNNEL FIRST."** [Source: TIM, ch. 19]

### The seven internal brand metrics
For evaluating an internal brand programme, not market effect:
1. **Business understanding** — how well employees understand the philosophical and historical foundations of the organisation, **how it makes money, who its customers are, and what the year's financial goals are.**
2. **Brand understanding** — how well the valuable and differentiated brand elements are articulated.
3. **Brand influence** — how well it is defined, communicated and sustained **in which specific ways employees influence the customer experience.**
4. **Brand trust** — employees' trust in leadership's ability to act rightly in the spirit of the brand values.
5. **Brand credibility** — whether employees believe that the company **can** keep its promises to customers **and employees.**
6. **Brand delivery** — whether they believe that it **actually keeps them.**
7. **Brand preference, advocacy, satisfaction** — whether employees would rather work here than at the competitor and **whether they would refer friends and family to their employer**, plus retention, turnover and satisfaction with the assimilation process itself.
Collection routes: employee focus groups, internal surveys, mentoring, performance reviews, **monitored suggestion boxes for informal feedback**, and detailed questionnaires at the end of each brand training.
**And the demand to go beyond standard measures: "Insightful metrics must go BEYOND the traditional measures of awareness and recall in order to address such issues as PRICE PREMIUMS, INTENT TO PURCHASE, and RETURNS ON BRAND INVESTMENT."** [Source: KOB, ch. 12]

## Decision criteria and heuristics

- **Determine the most important quantity first: "The most important thing to figure out is THE LIFETIME VALUE OF A CUSTOMER."** Without it, no channel decision is possible. [Source: TIM, ch. 19]
- **Control group instead of before-and-after.** In all four KOB families of measurement the comparison is against a **non-exposure group**; the ROCI table sets WITH and WITHOUT the communication programme side by side. **Without a comparison quantity none of the values is interpretable.** [Source: KOB, ch. 7 and 13]
- **Ad recall is the weakest, brand recall the stronger measure of learning**, because people cannot trace the origin of their knowledge. [Source: KOB, ch. 7]
- **For established brands you measure memory, not persuasion.** Neither attitude nor preference measures respond to one or a few exposures; what remains meaningful is **top-of-mind awareness** as a consideration-set indicator. [Source: KOB, ch. 7]
- **Expose briefly when perception is measured; letting people think at length distorts.** And focus groups are explicitly unsuitable for design evaluation. [Source: KOB, ch. 2]
- **For internal programmes the metric must be tied to incentives, otherwise it has no effect.** Evidence **Itron**: one metric linked brand building to **profit sharing** — "**employees created a plan for how they would live the brand, essentially setting up A CONTRACT with senior management for achieving the goal. At the end of the year, they were evaluated against this contract to determine what level of profit sharing they would receive.**" Second internal metric: regular measurement of **employee morale**, "based on the belief that brand-building initiatives should create a positive shift in employees' attitudes about and belief in the company." [Source: KOB, ch. 12]
- **Do not claim causality for external financial metrics.** Itron tied share-price development to brand building, **but with an explicit caveat: "it's generally difficult to isolate the role of brand in achieving stock price and profitability gain."** The result figures (share price from **3.70 to over 18.50 USD**, market capitalisation from **200 to 388 million USD**) are therefore to be read as context, not as proof of effect. [Source: KOB, ch. 12]
- **In brand valuation, lay open the two judgement parameters:** the brand's share of intangible earnings and the discount rate. In a standard durable-goods case they were **25 percent** and **15 percent**; the BVA range runs from **1 percent for commodities to 50–70 percent for luxury.** [Source: KOB, ch. 13]
- **Permission has its own, unusual metric: "How many people would REACH OUT AND WONDER (OR COMPLAIN) if you didn't send out that next email blast? That's a metric worth measuring and increasing."** [Source: TIM, ch. 17]
- **And a metric for traction that is not a number: "Who would MISS it if it were gone?"** Plus the operational test questions for a young offering: How many people outside headquarters use it **daily**? How often do they send suggestions for improvement? How many insist that friends and colleagues use it — **now**? [Source: TIM, ch. 8]

## Typical problems and approaches

**"Is brand advertising effect measurable?" — THE MAIN CONTRADICTION, deliberately left open.**
**TIM's position:** "Brand marketing is culturally oriented. **And it cannot be measured.**" Hence the instruction: refuse to measure, be patient and consistent, and if you cannot do that, do not run ads. Justified by fragmentation, indifference to advertising ("the most ignored advertising ever created"), unearned media and the cost calculation. [Source: TIM, ch. 15]
**KOB's position:** a complete canon of methods with four families, each with named limits of validity, control groups and the plea for implicit measures. [Source: KOB, ch. 7]
**What makes the contradiction special: KOB itself supplies the explanation of why TIM's refusal is plausible in practice.** The recall-underestimation finding ("people often have difficulty tracing the origin of their knowledge") and the establishment finding ("it is unlikely that one or even several exposures will affect people's judgments") are exactly the reasons TIM gives. **But KOB draws the opposite conclusion from them: not to stop measuring, but to measure differently** — brand recall instead of ad recall, implicit instead of direct, control group instead of before-and-after.
**Both positions are tenable and answer different questions:** TIM writes for actors without a research budget, for whom measurement would cost more than the insight gained; KOB for brand managers with access to copy-test service providers and reaction-time procedures.
**A possible synthesis, explicitly as a synthesis and not as a decision:** The line does not run between "measurable" and "not measurable", but between **measures that presuppose a control group and a laboratory** (KOB's territory) and **measures that can be read off behaviour in ongoing operation** (TIM's territory: click, order, repeat purchase, referral, being missed). Whoever has no access to the first should, according to both books, not pretend to have it — TIM calls this "refuse to measure", KOB calls it the limit of validity of attitude measures. **Both sides must remain defensible from the knowledge file.** See the contradiction register.

**"Our campaign is remembered well but does not sell."** Two diagnoses from KOB: (a) **brand linkage** is missing — the message is remembered but attributed to the wrong brand (Eveready/Duracell). (b) The recall is **negatively connoted in content** — the tool kit is remembered because it is associated with frequent breakdowns. **In both cases more recall is the wrong target quantity.** [Source: KOB, ch. 7]

**"The board wants a number for the brand."** The three pathways answer different questions and are not interchangeable: pathway 1 says **what customers know and feel**; pathway 2, **what short-term financial contribution the brand communication makes**; pathway 3, **what the brand is worth as an asset.** Whoever asks for "the brand value" usually means pathway 3, but for steering purposes needs pathways 1 and 2. [Source: KOB, ch. 13]

**"Why don't we invest more in the brand?"** The answer lies not in measurement technique but in the incentive structure: **"Virtually all of a brand's value resides in the future ... LESS THAN 5 PERCENT of the value resides in the first year."** And: "**The benefits are difficult to quantify, uncertain, and in the future. The costs are quantifiable, certain, and immediate.**" **That is why a manager who optimises for the short term and damages the brand is often rewarded.** [Source: KOB, introduction — details in `pricing`]

**"Our numbers look good, the business does not."** The diagnostic case is the BrandDynamics example: **Presence +35, but Advantage −12 and Bonding −14.** Awareness rises, attachment falls — a state that pure awareness measurement does not make visible. [Source: KOB, ch. 13]

**"We measure everything and still know nothing."** Godin's diagnosis of the measurement rate: risen from under 5 to about 60 percent, **"what's missing is a THOUGHTFUL ANALYSIS OF WHAT THOSE NUMBERS MEAN."** [Source: TIM, ch. 19] And KOB's counterpart at brand level: only a third of companies measure brand performance at all. [Source: KOB, ch. 12]

## Concrete examples
- **The Tiffany/Wal-Mart experiment as a measurement design** (not only as a finding): three groups, identical product, **the only manipulation is the brand mark.** Results **550 / 873 / 81 USD.** That is the simplest method documented in the system for measuring brand value in isolation. [Source: KOB, introduction]
- **The BMW one-versus-ten-reasons test** as an example that **processing fluency** itself has to be measured and not only the content: the ad with "can you think of ONE reason?" produced a **more favourable** attitude than the one with ten. [Source: KOB, ch. 7]
- **Yoyodyne's permission figures as a comparison anchor:** open rates **above 70 percent**, average response rate **33 percent** — "about **a thousand times** the rate of a typical commercial email sent in 2018." [Source: TIM, ch. 17]
- **HugDug as a documented measurement case with a clear negative result:** thousands of pages built (trial was there), **but fewer than six users built more than a dozen pages** — and a typical page sold **zero books a month.** The number that was missing was the whales. [Source: TIM, ch. 19]
- **The contribution chart of customer profitability:** fixed-cost line against gross profit per customer — **only eight of about thirty customers contributed any profit at all.** Valid for book buyers, restaurant guests, political donors, philanthropists, stamp collectors "and just about any industry where some customers spend more than others." [Source: TIM, ch. 14]
- **The development of advertising spending as a counter-finding to the "death of advertising": in 2004 advertising spending grew by about 8 percent, internet and cable advertising by double digits** — "it appears that the forecasted demise of advertising runs afoul of fact." [Source: KOB, ch. 7]
- **Northwestern Memorial HealthCare as an example of measuring operational results after a culture programme:** admissions **up 34 percent**, emergency department visits **up 27 percent**, births **up 47 percent** — and at the same time "**among the LOWEST-COST teaching hospitals**". [Source: KOB, ch. 18]
- **LEED certifications as a measurement of diffusion:** at launch **only two buildings per day** were submitted; "**at that rate, it would have taken A HUNDRED YEARS to hit the numbers they hit after just TWELVE.**" [Source: TIM, ch. 19]

## Open points (contradiction register)
The following points are **documented, not resolved.** Full evidence in the register in `../research/system-overview.md`. For requests that touch them, **both positions** are to be presented.

- **A2 Is brand advertising effect measurable? The main contradiction of this area.** Noteworthy: KOB itself supplies the findings that make TIM's position plausible, but draws from them the conclusion to measure differently rather than to stop. A possible synthesis is named in the register and kept explicitly as a synthesis, not as a decision.
- **B4 Internal work versus advertising effect** (KOB ch. 12 versus ch. 7).
- **Data currency as an open point:** no digital attribution, no incrementality tests, no valuation standard after 2005 (ISO 10668 is missing).
