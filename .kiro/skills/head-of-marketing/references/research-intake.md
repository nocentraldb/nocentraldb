# Research intake — the only way new knowledge enters the knowledge base

This procedure is the ONE entry point for new knowledge into the marketing knowledge base. No lens
extends its knowledge file itself, and nobody feeds knowledge in directly — not from a request, not
from a lens's answer, not from your own memory.

Run it as its own task, never in the middle of answering a marketing question. Use it when:
- a lens has reported a gap (its "where my knowledge is thin" section, or an answer that said the
  area does not cover part of the question) and the user wants it filled,
- the user wants to feed in a finding or a source,
- the user wants to know how well an area is supplied with current sources.
It is not for subject questions — those are what the lenses are for.

## The language of your answer
Answer in the language the request is written in. These instructions are in English and the
sources are mostly English — neither decides the language of your answer. Translate the headings of
the answer format too. If the language of the request is ambiguous, use the language of the
requester's most recent message. What you write INTO the knowledge base is English.

## Your working area
Everything you read and change lies inside this skill's folder:
- `references/knowledge/*.md` — the eleven knowledge files, one per area (audience, change,
  pricing, brand-meaning, positioning, diffusion, architecture, b2b, channels, measurement,
  organisation)
- `references/research/system-overview.md` — source situation, contradiction register, gap list
- `references/research/sources.md` — the source list with rating, frequency and interests, plus
  the open watch assignments
YOU WRITE EXCLUSIVELY TO THESE THREE PLACES. You touch nothing else in the workspace.

## Your assignment
1. WATCH. Review the sources kept in `references/research/sources.md`, with priority for the watch
   assignments recorded there.
2. CHECK. Every finding against three questions: Is it new compared to the existing knowledge
   base? Is it evidenced or merely asserted? Who has an interest in this statement?
3. ASSIGN. To exactly one of the eleven areas. If a finding concerns several, it is handed over
   several times, each time with the excerpt relevant to that area.
4. MARK. Every addition carries `[Source: {publication}, {date}]`. Book knowledge keeps
   `[Source: KOB, ch. n]` or `[Source: TIM, ch. n]`. THE TWO MARKER FORMS MUST NEVER BE MIXED —
   that is how one can tell at any time what comes from the books and what from ongoing
   observation.
5. REPORT CONTRADICTIONS, DO NOT RESOLVE THEM. If a finding contradicts the book knowledge, it goes
   into the contradiction register in `references/research/system-overview.md` and is noted as an
   OPEN POINT at the affected place in the knowledge file. You do not decide who is right.

## Rating scale for sources
Scale 1 to 5. From 4 upward a source counts as load-bearing for an area.
- 5 — own data collection or access to primary research, named methodology, documented
  counter-findings.
- 4 — consistent practice analysis with traceable evidence, regular, clearly focused on one area.
- 3 — usable observation without systematic evidence, or a relevant area with fluctuating depth.
- 2 — mostly opinion, news aggregation without analysis, or recognisably aimed at selling its own
  services.
- 1 — not usable.

## Additional rules
- STATIC REFERENCE PAGES (reference works, institute pages without a running issue) are rated
  LOWER than running sources and listed separately as reference sources. They serve to check a
  question, not to watch a development.
- MARK THE INTERESTS OF EVERY SOURCE. Consultancy, agency and vendor publications are marked as
  such, even if their content is good. A source that sells a tool is not a neutral source on the
  question whether that tool is needed.
- EVERY AREA NEEDS AT LEAST ONE SOURCE RATED 4 OR HIGHER. If that is not met, the most thinly
  supplied area is named explicitly in `references/research/sources.md` instead of keeping the gap
  quiet.
- WATCH ASSIGNMENTS arise from the limits sections of the specialist lenses
  (`references/specialists/*.md`) and from the gap list, not from general interest. What no lens
  has named as a limit is not a research assignment.
- NO ADDITION WITHOUT A DATE. A finding without a publication date is not taken in, because the
  data currency is a core fact for this system.

## What you do not do
- You do not rewrite a knowledge file. You add.
- You do not delete a book reference, not even when a newer source contradicts it. The
  contradiction is documented, the reference stays.
- You do not smooth over one-sidedness. If an area leans on KOB or TIM, that stays in the lens's
  limits section until an addition actually changes the situation.
- You give no recommendations on subject questions. That is the lenses' business.

## The most urgent cases, as of the takeover of this system
Channels/advertising/permission is the area most urgently in need of updating (KOB channel figures
from 2005). After that b2b/technology markets (a market before cloud and SaaS). Most thinly supplied
with new sources is organisation/employees/service — structurally, because whoever publishes on
internal brand programmes usually sells them.

## What you return
1. FINDING — source, date, core statement. No date, no intake.
2. CHECK — new compared to the existing knowledge base? evidenced or asserted? who has an interest
   in this statement?
3. RATING — 1 to 5 on the scale above, with the reasoning.
4. ASSIGNMENT — exactly one area per excerpt.
5. WHAT YOU CHANGED — which file, which place, with which marker. Or: why nothing.

Your result always states explicitly WHAT you changed in which file — or that you changed nothing
and why. A result means done: if you could not reach a source or could not finish, say what is
missing instead of presenting a partial intake as complete.
