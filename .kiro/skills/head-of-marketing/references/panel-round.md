# The whole-of-marketing panel

The whole marketing panel at once. Use it when a question is broad and you do not know which areas
it touches — a brand presence as a whole, a product launch, a strategy that touches everything from
positioning to price. All eleven lenses look at it independently of one another; what comes back is
ONE recommendation, sorted by leverage. For a question whose area you know, a single lens is faster
and sharper (see SKILL.md, "Two ways, and when to use which").

## Who sits on the panel
All eleven lenses, each with its specialist file and its knowledge file:

brand-meaning, positioning, audience, change, diffusion, architecture, channels, pricing,
measurement, organisation, b2b — paths `references/specialists/<lens>.md` and
`references/knowledge/<lens>.md`.

## Before the panel
Write the dossier first (SKILL.md, "The dossier"). Without it the panel guesses what the question is
about, and you get eleven lectures instead of findings.

## Running the panel
Delegate each of the eleven lenses to its OWN sub-agent, in parallel. Each sub-agent is handed only:
- the request and the dossier,
- the path of its one specialist file and its one knowledge file, with the instruction to read
  both before answering,
- the panel answer format below.
It is NOT shown the other lenses' answers, your draft verdict, or the list of other lenses. The
lenses must not see each other — independence is the point of the panel: a finding several lenses
reach on their own is the most valuable kind of finding a panel produces, and it only means
something if they could not copy it from each other.

The panel answer format to hand each sub-agent:
- CONCERNS ME: yes | no — if "no", the answer ends after at most one sentence on what this lens
  could contribute if the question were cut differently. No courtesy paragraph, no opinion from the
  neighbouring area. On the panel, a "no" is as useful as a "yes".
- FINDINGS: sorted by weight, the most important first, each with its location in the material
  (file and line, or the quoted sentence). No finding without a location.
- RECOMMENDATION: sorted by weight, the most important first, concrete and executable. Where text
  is concerned, the written proposal, not a description of it.
- UNCERTAIN: only if it would change the recommendation. Otherwise omit.
- Answer in the language of the original request.

If you cannot delegate to sub-agents, apply the eleven lenses yourself one after the other, and
write down each lens's findings before you open the next lens's files, so an earlier lens does not
colour a later one. If a sub-agent does not return, fold what you have; if the missing lens could
change the recommendation, say under "What I won't commit to" which area was not assessed.

## Folding the answers into one recommendation
In front of you are independent assessments by marketing specialists of one question. Each has its
own area, each answered independently, none saw the others' answers. You put them together into
ONE recommendation.

The recipient has the decision to make. They want to know what to change and in which order. They
do not want to know what is in some books.

### What you throw away — without mention, without counting, without a gap note
- Every contribution in which the lens declares itself not concerned — in whatever words and in
  whatever language, whether as its first line or as a sentence somewhere. Lenses opting out is the
  normal case, not a finding.
- Every paragraph that reports what a source says without changing the action.
- Every borrowed case study — TiVo, Palm Pilot, Pepsi Challenge, Starbucks and their kind. A
  foreign case from a foreign market does not explain what is to be done HERE. Only if an analogy
  actually carries a recommendation does it survive — as a subordinate clause, never as a
  paragraph.
- Every finding without a location in the material when another contribution delivers the same
  finding WITH one. With duplicates, the evidenced version wins.

### How you put it together
1. THE VERDICT — two to four sentences: what is the situation, what is to be done? Whoever reads
   only this paragraph must be able to act. It comes first, not at the end.
2. WHAT TO CHANGE — a numbered list, sorted by leverage, not by area and not by order of arrival.
   Every point carries its location in the material. Where a lens supplied a text proposal, you
   take it over verbatim; if there are two proposals for the same place, you choose the better one
   and say in half a sentence why.
   Merge what several found independently: if three areas arrive at the same finding, that is ONE
   point with greater weight — and you say that it was reached independently several times. That
   is the most valuable kind of finding a panel produces.
3. WHAT CAN BE LEFT ALONE — short, with the reason. What several lenses explicitly consider
   secondary, or what only pays off after another decision.
4. WHAT THERE IS NO COMMITMENT ON — only what changes the recommendation. If two lenses contradict
   each other in such a way that the RECOMMENDATION depends on it, decide: say which position you
   follow and why, and name the other in one sentence. Contradictions that do not change the action
   you leave out entirely. A contradiction left open is not an answer but work handed back.

### What you do not do
- You do not invent evidence, a chapter reference or a location that is in no contribution. You
  add nothing from your own knowledge at this step.
- You do not report on the process: who answered, who opted out, how many there were. The
  recipient is not interested.
- You do not smooth over an inconvenient recommendation and do not drop a finding because it is
  awkward.
- You do not build a section about the make-up of the sources. If a data-currency problem affects a
  concrete recommendation, it stands as half a sentence next to that recommendation — nowhere else.

No preface, no greeting, no closing formula.

Answer in the language of the original request — NOT in the language of these instructions, and
not in the language of the contributions if that differs. Translate the section headings too.
